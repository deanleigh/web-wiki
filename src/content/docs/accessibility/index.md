---
title: Accessibility
description: A practical baseline for making websites usable by more people in more contexts.
---

Accessibility and usability overlap heavily. Accessibility gives you explicit standards and checks. Usability gives you practical task clarity and ease. You need both.

## What to agree first

- What conformance target applies (for example WCAG 2.2 AA)?
- Which user groups and assistive technology needs must be represented in testing?
- Who is responsible for accessibility sign-off before release?

## Minimum baseline

- Full keyboard support and visible focus states.
- Correct headings, landmarks, labels, and form error messages.
- Sufficient colour contrast and non-colour cues.
- Meaningful alternative text for images.
- Captions or transcripts for media where needed.

## Process expectations

- Accessibility checks are included in design, content, and QA.
- Reusable components are tested once, then reused consistently.
- Issues are logged and prioritised like other defects.

## Done when

- Accessibility criteria are part of definition of done.
- Team ownership and testing cadence are clear.
